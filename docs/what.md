# What topos is

bound's SDK: the wire, the forms, the contract and the shell you build a world with — a reader of any source or a
renderer of any artefact, in an afternoon.

The wire is the line. A lock is a file of lines, and a line is a header and its fields, written one way only, so that
parsing a line and writing it again gives back the same bytes. Its digest is taken of those bytes, and its signature of
the same bytes without the signature, never of anything a host added.

The forms are how a value is read: an interval with its floor and its ceiling, an alphabet of members, a ladder and the
rest, each read once, in the wire, and refused where it would break its form.

The contract is what a world owes: a request, and a fact, an abstention or a refusal — to render a page, to write a
receipt, to observe a source, to run. The shell hands a region only the lines it declares it reads, and a region handed
less than it declares is refused.

A world is a capsule built on them: its own lock, its code and its vectors. A reader of a source is one, a renderer of an
artefact is another, and topos knows neither. It produces no text and writes no order of its own: cells, meets, joins
and states come from the object, and the words, the markup and the figures of a page live in the world that renders
them. topos is what those worlds rest on, and what lets two of them read one line the same way.
